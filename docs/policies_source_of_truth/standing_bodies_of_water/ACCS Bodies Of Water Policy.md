RATIONALE.

The safety and well being of children is a priority for All Child Care Solutions

It has been recorded that in Ontario in 2010, there were 167 drowning deaths. Of these, 19 occurred in backyard swimming pools and eight involved children less than five years of age. The use of unregulated standing bodies of water (e.g. ponds), as well

as portable /”kiddie”/ inflatable wading type used in homes can pose a drowning risk to children of all ages.

The 2010 Chief Coroner’s Drowning review, as well as the 2012 Chief Coroner Inquest into the drowning death of Jeremie Audette, made recommendations regarding access to water by young children.

The purpose of this memorandum is to notify providers and their families of consistent, province wide requirements being implemented by the Ministry of Education regarding standing and recreational bodies of water (i.e. ponds, pools, etc.) in licensed private home day care.

The memorandum also outlines best practices regarding water safety for children in all regulated childcare setting.

All Child Care Solutions to comply with Ministry of Education requirement.

Effective immediately, licensed private home day care agencies (PHDC) must:

Not allow the use of and access to all standing bodies of water (i.e. ponds), recreational in-ground,

above-ground swimming pool, portable /”kiddy”/ inflatable wading type, and hydro massage pools, hot tubs,

and spas located on the premises of any single or multidwelling private residence.

Also including a provider’s own house, their neighbours home, townhouse complex or apartment

building where the provider lives.

Restriction for children under the supervision/care of the contracted private home day care provider in his/her

capacity as a child care provider during operating hours.(day or night or weekends)

no child under six years old will use or have access to any standing or recreational body of water on the premises;

no child six years old or older will use or have access to any standing or recreational body of water.

All Child Care Solutions is in compliance with the PHDC policy.

This ministry policy supersedes and replaces any previously issued directive(s) related to access to and use of water bodies and is applicable to all licensed PHDC agencies and their contracted private home day care providers in the province.

This policy supports and is an addition to the existing requirements for licensed PHDC agencies and individuals associated with these agencies as outlined in all relevant legislation, regulation, service guidelines and policy directives.

Ministry licensing staff will monitor and support compliances with requirements.

5.   Restrictions on providers and child visiting other homes.

Child (ren) may not go into a neighbours or friends back yard who have a pool, pond or any standing body of water. Providers are not allowed to bring children to visit any other person’s home unless that home has been inspected by the Agency first.

What are some safer water based activity alternatives?

Municipal by law.

Recreational water bodies other than public pools such as sprinklers, water tablets and splash pads are not regulated but are a lower risk-alternative. Public park wading pools on the other hand are inspected by public boards of Health per the Recreational Water Protocol, under the Safe Water Program of the Ontario Public Health Standards. The protocol is available at:

http://www.health.gov.on.ca/en/pro/programs/publichealth/oph_standards/docs/protocols_guidelines/Recreational_Water%20Protocol_2018_en.pdf

Municipal by law.

Does the new policy apply to unlicensed informal home-based childcare providers?

As the ministry does not have a licensing relationship under the Child Care and Early Years Act with informal care providers, the ministry does not have authority to direct the informal child care sector regarding the use of pools etc. on the premises of private residences used as informal child care settings. There are municipalities in the province that have by-laws regarding enclosures for pools in private homes.

Informal care providers living in such areas are subject to these by-laws where they exist.

For all childcare providers licensed or un licensed, preventing risk of drowning, injury and disease transmission for children should be a priority!
