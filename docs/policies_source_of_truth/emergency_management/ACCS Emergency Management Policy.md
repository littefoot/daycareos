Emergency Management Policy

Policy

Providers will follow the emergency response procedures outlined in this document by following these three phases:

1. Immediate Emergency Response;

2. Next Steps during an Emergency; and

3. Recovery.

Providers will ensure that children are kept safe, are accounted for and are supervised at all times during an emergency situation.

Evacuation Procedures

In the event of an emergency, such as fire, bomb threat, flood or any other situation in which there is immediate danger to being inside the home, The Provider and All Child Care Solutions Inc. will have designated the _______________________________ as the off-site location.

Responsibilities of Providers:

• Check all surroundings looking for potential hazards, reporting any findings to the home visitor.

• Account for all children.  Conduct a headcount and confirm with attendance records.

• Gather all medications and any other essential needs (eg. Epi-Pens, Asthma Inhalers, appropriate supplies to protect the children (blankets), etc)

• Once outside the home, conduct a headcount again and confirm with attendance records

• Remain calm and wait for further directions from the Agency.

• To reduce stress on children, provide distractions by holding circles, singing songs and playing games.

Responsibilities of Agency and/or Designate

• Notify emergency authorities, giving them as much information as possible

• Depending on the type of emergency, remind provider of their evacuation site they should go to

• Notify parents of circumstances as soon as you are able.

• Contact the parents with the estimated time of arrival of children and providers to evacuation site.

• Transport water, first-aid supplies, and emergency numbers on file for a backup.

• Ensure all children have been evacuated from the home.

• Follow directions given by police /emergency authorities once they have arrived.

Once you have safely evacuated the home to the on-site or off site location:

• Notify families of the situation and evacuation location for immediate pick-up of children.  (home visitor or designate responsible for calling families, etc.)  Make sure you are clear on what information is being given to families and that providers are relaying the same message.

• Determine where to set up different groups of children and define areas if possible.

• Document children being picked up and by whom.

Assisted Evacuation

One-on-one, or additional, help may be required by a child with special needs.

Tips on assisting children with special needs:

• Have specific knowledge of the child’s needs.

• Speak clearly and inform the child of what is happening and what you are going to do.  Once child is aware of how you will help them, proceed by assisting them in the appropriate manor (push/pull wheelchair, etc)

• To communicate with a deaf and blind person, draw an ‘X’ on their back or try tracing letters with your finger on the palm of their hand to let them know you are there to help during the emergency.

• Follow instructions posted on special needs equipment or assistive device during an emergency.

• Avoid attempts to lift, support or assist in moving someone unless you are familiar with safe techniques.

Sheltering

Sheltering involves keeping all windows and doors closed and covering air-intake vents to provide protection from airborne hazardous materials.  You would normally take shelter in the event of earthquakes, hurricanes, tornados, hazardous materials accidents, terrorist attacks, etc.

• Ensure all providers are in the home (outside play areas are unoccupied)

• Ensure all doors and windows are locked

• Turn off all exhaust fans in kitchens, bathrooms and any other spaces

• Use tape and plastic food wrapping to cover bathroom exhausts, vents, dryer vents and other openings to the outdoors.  Seal any obvious gaps around windows and doors.  Stay away from all windows and doors.

• Close the curtains for additional protection

• If the vapours begin to bother the providers and children, hold wet cloths over the nose and mouth.

• Remain in the home until notified by the emergency response authorities that the situation has been resolved.

Type of Emergency | Roles and Responsibilities for Providers

Snow and Ice Storms | During a snow or ice storm where you may need to close the daycare home, follow these steps:  • Discuss current and predicted weather situation with the agency for procedures and approval for closing or limited usage of the daycare home • Monitor winter storm warnings • Check status of battery powered radios, flashlights, back up lighting, power and heat • Consider discussing with the agency the possibility of closing the night before, however, if not possible, make sure to notify all parents/guardians immediately after the decision has been made to close the home daycare • If you know storms are approaching, request and confirm emergency contact information from parents/guardians when they drop off their children in the morning.  In case they cannot be reached, alternate designates should be contacted to pick up children. Remember: • If you are sending children home early due to weather conditions, call parents/guardians to make arrangements to have their children picked up as soon as possible • Leave information on your voicemail so that when parents call, they receive up to date information about the home.  • arrange for snow and ice removal, as well as possible debris, such as fallen trees and utility lines.

Lockdown | These lockdown procedures describe the steps that you should take to ensure the safety and security of providers and children during a violent incident. If the home is affected by threats, intruders, drive by shootings, active shooters etc.,  Close blinds and turn off all lights, leaving minimal lighting Ensure all doors are locked Make your way down to basement, lock and barricade the door Call 911call Alert the Agency about the current situation  Do not let anyone in, including parents Do not respond to anyone outside the home unless it’s a police official  Follow the serious occurrence reporting procedures     For the children that are outside the home and the threat is inside the home  Take all children to the nearest safe area away from the daycare home  Review attendance and make sure all children on the attendance is present  Call 911 Alert the Agency of your current location and situation  Call parents and inform them of your current situation and pick up point Follow the serious occurrence reporting procedures

Potentially Violent Situations | If a potentially violent individual gains access to your home and leaves: Call 911 immediately  Close all blinds, lock all doors and turn off all lights  Proceed to the storage room and wait for police officials to arrive If a potentially violent individual gains access to your home and remains: Call 911 immediately  Try to isolate the potential aggressor from as many adults and children as possible  Do not physically restrain an individual  Seek to draw the individual to another room or part of the home that has no children present  Once police arrive, allow them to handle the situation while you seek shelter with the rest of the children in another part of the home  Follow the serious occurrence reporting procedures

Disgruntled or Impaired parents/guardians or disruptive Visitors or Unauthorized Representatives | If a parent, guardian, family member or visitor of the home engages in disruptive or potentially dangerous behaviour, the person should be asked to leave the home immediately  If you have reasonable cause to suspect that any person who has come into your home, picking up a child is under the influence of alcohol, drugs or is emotionally impaired in any way and may endanger a child or is disruptive and potentially dangerous  Alert the Agency and other family members of the situation  Remain polite and calm  If the person appears to be under the influence of alcohol or drugs, offer to call a taxi and pay for the taxi If the person becomes agitated or confrontational, call 911 If the disgruntles or intoxicated parent/guardian is demanding their child be released to them, try to stall as long as possible until the police arrive.  If necessary follow the serious occurrence reporting procedure

Hostage Situations | If involved in a hostage situation with disgruntled parents/guardians, authorized representatives or anyone else Remain calm and polite Call 911 or instruct someone else to do so Follow the hostage-taker’s instructions Try to alert all family members of the situation and for them to close all doors  Follow the directions of the police

Missing Child | When a child has gone out of sight for longer than 30 seconds, except where providers know exactly where the child is (washroom) Inform the Agency and alert other family members immediately Conduct a search of all areas of the home, including closets, etc Call the parent  Call 911 Continue searching  If the child is missing and depending on severity of the situation, file a serious occurrence report

Infectious Disease, Outbreaks and Pandemics | Infectious diseases are diseases that are transmitted from person-to-person. Some examples of potential outbreaks include: salmonella, e-coli, the flu, etc Daily observation of the child should be carried out by the providers  Assess each child for the following symptoms or illness: Fever >38 C (100 F), flushing, pallor or illness Acute cold, nasal discharge or coughing  Vomiting or diarrhea  Red or discharged eyes or ears Yellowish skin or eyes Unusual spots or rashes  Unusual behaviour (irritability, fussiness, restless) If any of these symptoms appear: Isolate the ill child from other children  Notify parents promptly and request that they take the child home *Report immediately all communicable diseases to Agency In the event of an outbreak: Providers will increase disinfecting of cribs, cots, toys and equipment  Providers and children will be directed to increase their hand washing practices. Liquid soap will be in contact for 30-60 seconds.

Bomb Threats, threatening call or message, Hold and Secure | Any threat should be treated as real until proven otherwise  Stay calm and do not argue The provider that received the call should try and tell the Agency that a bomb threat is in progress so that other providers (if in a building) can evacuate immediately Providers should call 911 immediately Follow the serious occurrence reporting procedure

Disaster Requiring Evacuation: Floods, Power Failure, Fire | You may need to evacuate providers and children from the home in the event of a fire, bomb threat, flood or any other situation where there is immediate danger. Depending on the circumstances, you will need to decide to evacuate to the off-site location. Steps to follow when getting ready for evacuation: Quickly scan the space around you looking for any potential hazards Account for all children. Conduct a head count and ensure accurate attendance records; attendance sheet must be kept with the providers Gather all medications (epi-pen, puffer, etc.)  Take children’s emergency cards Notify landlord of circumstances as soon as you are able (if in a rental)  Upon arrival at the off-site location, contact all parents and let them know the situation and the pickup location

Fire | A fire evacuation plan is provided for each home to show all the fire exists and the location of the off-site location.  If you discover a small fire (waste paper basket size) extinguish the fire by using water, blanket, fire extinguisher, etc. If you discover a larger fire: Leave the fire area closing all doors behind you, in order to confine the fire Pull the fire alarm to notify everyone about the fire Call 911 Proceed to follow the evacuation steps, even in the case of the fire alarm going off

Floods | Floods can result from snowmelt, ice jams, heavy spring rains, summer thunderstorms, tropical storms or hurricanes Listen for announcements of flood watches and warnings Inspect areas prone to floods such as the basement  Pay attention to evacuation orders from public officials Do not walk or drive through areas that are deeper that knee-high

Power Failure | In the event of a power failure: Notify landlord (if in a rental) Check other homes and apartments around your home to see if others are without power. If there are power lines down, report it and do not go near downed power lines Due to the extreme risk of fire, do not use candles during a power outage buy flashlights  Leave the light on so that you know when the power has been turned back on

Natural Disaster: Earthquakes, Tornados, Hurricanes | In case a earthquake occurs providers should: Remain calm Help children find shelter under a sturdy desk or table and away from unstable structures Ensure everyone is away from windows Find shelter for themselves Wait for the shaking to stop *Providers who are outdoors with children must immediately ensure that everyone stays away from power lines, trees, and other tall structures that may collapse and wait for shaking to stop *Once shaking stops, providers must Gather the children, their emergency cards and any  medications      Tornado - In case of a tornado, the providers that become aware of the tornado or tornado warning must inform all other providers immediately by notifying the Agency  Providers must remain calm Gather all children and go to the basement (in houses) Take children’s attendance to ensure all children are present  Keep children calm Wait for further instructions

Disaster- External: Gas leak, oil spill, chemical release, forest fire, nuclear emergency | An incident outside of the home that may have adverse effects on persons in the home E.g. gas leak, oil spill, chemical release, nuclear emergency The providers who becomes aware of the external environmental threat must inform Agency of the threat as quickly and safely as possible. Seal off external air entryways located in the program rooms Place a note on all external doors with instructions that no one may enter or exit the home until further notice  Continue normal operations while waiting for further instructions

Boil Water Advisory | Possible microbiological contamination would warrant a BWA (Boil Water Advisory) because boiling can destroy microorganisms. In an event of a BWA, follow the steps below: Post notices that state “Do not drink or use tap water” Ensure the water is made safe to drink by boiling at a rolling boil for at least one minute Ensure all adults in the home and children do not consume untreated tap water Notify all parents of the advisory and the procedures taken

Procedures to Follow When “All-Clear” Notification is Given | Procedures to Follow When “All-Clear” Notification is Given

Procedures | The provider or family member who receives the ‘all-clear’ from an authority must inform the Agency that the ‘all-clear’ has been given and that it is safe to return to the home.  Providers who have assisted individuals with medical and/or special needs with exiting the home will assist and accompany these individuals with the assistance of a family member or agency home visitor.    Providers must: take attendance to ensure all children are accounted for; escort children back to home take attendance upon returning to the home to ensure that all children are accounted for; where applicable; and re-open closed/sealed blinds, windows and doors.  The Agency will determine if operations will resume and communicate this decision to Providers.

Communication with parents/ guardians | As soon as possible, the Home Visitor/Agency designate must notify parents/guardians of the emergency situation and that the all-clear has been given.  Where disasters have occurred that did not require evacuation of the home, the Agency must provide a notice of the incident to parents/guardians by.  If normal operations do not resume the same day that an emergency situation has taken place, the Agency must provide parents/guardians with information as to when and how normal operations will resume as soon as this is determined.

Procedures to Follow When “Unsafe to Return” Notification is Given | Procedures to Follow When “Unsafe to Return” Notification is Given

Procedures | The provider or family member who receives the ‘unsafe to return’ notification from an authority must inform the Agency of this direction and to proceed from the meeting place to the evacuation site, or the site determined by emergency services personnel.  Providers must take attendance to confirm that all children are accounted for, and escort children to the evacuation site.  Providers who have assisted individuals with medical and/or special needs with exiting the home will assist and accompany these individuals to the evacuation site with the Agency designate.   The Agency - All Child Care Solutions Inc. will post a note for parents/guardians on the home entrance with information on the evacuation site, where it is possible and safe to do so.  Upon arrival at the evacuation site, providers must: remain calm; take attendance to ensure all children are accounted for; help keep children calm;  engage children in activities, where possible; conduct ongoing visual checks and head counts of children; maintain constant supervision of the children;  keep attendance as children are picked up by their parents, guardians or authorized pick-up persons; and remain at the evacuation site until all children have been picked up.

Communication with parents/ guardians | 1) Upon arrival at the emergency evacuation site, the Agency will notify parents/guardians of the emergency situation, evacuation and the location to pick up their children.  2)  Where possible, the Provider will update their voicemail as soon as possible to inform parents/guardians that the home has been evacuated, and include the details of the evacuation site location and contact information in the message.

Procedures for Resuming Normal Operations | • If possible, reopen home • If necessary, find temporary location  • Contact parents via email or phone  • Contact any volunteers and students via email  • Contact Ministry of Education Program Advisor  • If necessary; respond to media and community inquires  • Contact insurance company if necessary

Procedures for Providing Support to Children and Providers who Experience Distress | If providers, volunteers, students or children experience stress related to an emergency event at All Child Care Solutions Inc., the home visitor will provide individuals or parents/ guardians with the contact information for Toronto Public Health to access appropriate mental health supports & services. Procedures for Debriefing Providers, Children and Parents/ Guardians | The Agency must debrief providers, children and parents/guardians after the emergency.  The Agency will prepare a memo providing details of the emergency situation and next steps for the home to resume regular service to clients. This memo will be given to all parents/guardians, providers, volunteers and students.
