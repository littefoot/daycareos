Hand Washing
• Appropriate hand hygiene is one of the most important protective strategies. It is
suggested that home care providers and visitors should be trained and able to assist
children on appropriate hand hygiene, including the use of alcohol-based hand rub
(ABHR), and reinforcing its use.
• It is suggested that ABHR with a minimum 60% alcohol concentration be available
(60-90% recommended, ideally at the entry point to each child care room) and/or
plain liquid soap in dispensers, sinks and paper towels in dispensers. ABHR is most
effective when hands are not visibly soiled. It is recommended that ABHR only be
used for children ages two and up, and with adult supervision.
• Proper hand hygiene should be conducted by anyone entering the child care setting
and incorporated into the daily schedule at regular intervals during the day, above
and beyond what is usually recommended (e.g., before eating food, after using the
washroom, etc.)
• Soap and water are preferred as the most effective and safest method for hand
hygiene.

Pick-Up and Drop-Off
1. Avoid all physical contact with parents – No handshakes hugs etc
2. Monitor children’s temperature:
a) On arrival at the home, and any time a child shows symptoms of illness.
b) The Home Visitor/Director/Provider will print the attached Daily Health Check Form to monitor the
temperature of all children throughout the day.
c) NOTE: The Provider must use the Record of Illness Form – Individual to record information
(symptoms, age, room, etc.) concerning an ill child.
3. Ask parents to drop-off and pick-up their children at staggered intervals (to avoid interaction).
4. Parents should drop-off children at the main entrance and have the provider bring the child into their
home. Parents may wait outside the main entrance at pick-up time.
5. Designate an area near the main entrance to conduct screening. The area should allow for physical
distancing (minimum 2 meters) between provider and the person being screened. If physical distancing
cannot be maintained, providers conducting screening should wear appropriate personal protective
equipment, such as mask and gloves. Alcohol based hand rub (ABHR) containing at least 60-90% alcohol
content should be available at the screening station for hand hygiene.
6. Actively screen parents and children, prior to entry/drop-off for respiratory symptoms. A central record
of monitoring should be kept. Examples of screening questions include, but not limited to the following:
a) Do you or the child or any member of your household have any of the following symptoms:
Enhanced Preventive Procedures for Providers (Up to date screening sheet posted near entrance)
• Fever
• New or existing cough
• Difficulty breathing
• Sore throat
• Vomiting and/or diarrhea?
b) Have you or the child had close contact in the last 14 days with a person with acute respiratory illness?
c) Have you or the child had close contact (within 2 meters) in the last 14 days with someone that is a
confirmed/ probable case of COVID-19 (including those who are waiting for COVID testing result)?
d) Have you or the child traveled outside of Canada, within the last 14 days?
7. Anyone who answers YES to any of the question should not be permitted to enter the provider's home.
8. Make ABHR available at the screening stations for people who have answered NO to all questions for
use prior to entry into the home. When ABHR is used on children they must be supervised. To ensure

To ensure all staff are aware of the expectations regarding the supervision of children. 
The Child Care and Early Years Act (2014) identifies the following references for 
supervision of children: 
Supervision by an Adult 
Every licensee shall ensure that every child who receives child care at a child care 
centre it operates, or at a premise where it oversees the provision of home child care, is 
supervised by an adult at all times, whether the child is on or off the premi ses. 
Intent: 
This provision protects the safety and  well-being of children, by requiring that they be 
supervised by an adult at all times while receiving child care. 
Group Sizes 
Effective September 1, 2020, cohort sizes reflect that maximum group sizes set out 
under the Child Care and Early Years Act, 2014 (CCEYA). Staff and students are not 
included in group sizes but should be assigned to a specific group where possible. 
Children are permitted to attend on a part-time basis and as with children attending full 
time, should be included in one group and should not mix with other groups. 
Movement of supervisors or designates, staff and students on educational placement 
between child care locations
 and between licensed age groups is permitted. In the 
event of an outbreak, all movement  of supervisors or designates, staff and students 
should cease until the outbreak is over. Reducing the movement of staff and placement 
students where possible is encouraged  to minimize potential for transmission.

<!-- Page 17 -->
R
atios set out under the CCEYA must be maintained. Reduced ratios are permitted as 
set out under the CCEYA, provided that groups are not mixed. Reduced ratios are not 
permitted at any time for infants. 
Maximum capacity rules do not apply to Special Needs Resource staff on site (i.e. if 
they are not counted towards staff to child ratios, they are not included in the maximum 
capacity rules). 
Mixed age grouping is permitted as set out under the CCEYA where a director approval 
has been granted on the licence. 
While on duty, staff are responsible for the safety of the children at all times and 
constant supervision is required. Staff are assigned responsibility for a specific group of 
children. Staff must determine each child’s developmental ability (using developmental 
checklist materials) in order to plan and implement a program that effectively meets the 
child’s individual needs. Planning the program with consideration given to the children’s 
abilities as well as general safety practices is an essential component of effective 
supervision of children. 
Space Set-Up and Physical Distancing 
• Cohorts must have their own assigned indoor space separated from other cohorts
by a physical barrier. The physical barrier needs to be a minimum of 8 feet and as
wide as the room will allow.
• When in common spaces a physical distancing of at least 2 metres must be
maintained between different groups.
• Low contact activities are permitted indoors. For children in grade 1 and up,
masking is encouraged but not required if a minimum of two metres distance can
be maintained between groups and as much as possible within a group.
• High contact physical activities should take place in outdoor settings only.
Masking is not required outdoors for high contact physical activities.
• Singing is permitted indoors; masking is encouraged but not required for singing
indoors if a minimum of two metres distance can be maintained between cohorts
and as much distancing as possible maintained within a cohort.
• In shared outdoor space, groups must maintain a distance of at least 2 metres
between groups and any other individuals outside of the cohort.
The risk associated with transmission with shared objects is low. Instead of regular 
cleaning of shared objects, the focus should be on regular hand hygiene and respiratory 
etiquette to reduce the risk of infection related to shared equipment.  
Large playground equipment does not require disinfection.
