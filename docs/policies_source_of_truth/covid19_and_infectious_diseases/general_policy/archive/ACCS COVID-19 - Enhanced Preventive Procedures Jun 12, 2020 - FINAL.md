Enhanced Preventive Procedures 
Should All Child Care Solutions Inc. be required to continue to operate, there are additional 
enhanced preventive procedures which will be put in place to further reduce the risk of infection. 
The enhanced preventive procedures which the Director, home visitor and home provider will follow is 
dependent on the following guiding principles: 
• Exclusion
• Open Communication between Providers and Families
• Use of Personal Protective Equipment
• Changes to Daily Routines to Minimize the Spread of Infection
o Monitoring, Social Distancing and Cleaning and Disinfection Procedures
• Agency team Structure
• Home Visitor & Director's Role

Additional Protocols When an Individual Demonstrates Symptoms of Illness or Becomes Sick at 
the Provider's Home 
1. If a separate room is not available, the sick person should be kept at a minimum of 2 meters from
others.
2. The sick person should be provided with tissues and reminded of hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette,
and proper disposal of tissues.
3. If the sick person is a child, provider should remain with the child until a parent arrives. If tolerated
and above the age of 2, the child should wear a surgical mask. The childcare provider should wear a
surgical mask and eye protection ǁhile ǁaiting. The childcare provider should also avoid contact
with the child’s respiratory secretions. Dust ǁear gloves iĨ child needs attending to͘
4. All items used by the sick person should be cleaned and disinfected. All items that cannot be cleaned
(paper, books, cardboard puzzles) should be removed and stored in a sealed container for a minimum of
7 days.

Daily Routines and Procedures for ProviderƐ & Volunteers
Practice social distancing and include cleaning and disinfection as a very important part of your daily routines. 
Opening 
1. Provider must always wear a protective face mask when working at the home, this is for their own 
protection and the protection of the children and parents. The following resources will help providers 
to make their own face mask if they are unable to obtain a proper face mask:
a) https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tPx1yqvJgf4&feature=youtu.be.
b) https://www.cdc.gov/coronavirus/2019-ncov/prevent-getting-sick/diy-cloth-face-
coverings.html.
2. Ensure that the playroom (including play materials) has been cleaned, disinfected and ready to use.
Pick-Up and Drop-Off 
1. Avoid all physical contact with parents – No handshakes͕ hugs etc͘
2. Monitor children’s temperature:
a) On arrival at the home, and any time a child shows symptoms of illness.
b) The Home Visitor/Director will print the attached Daily Health Check Form to monitor the 
temperature of all children at three evenly spaced times throughout the day.
c) NOTE: The Provider must use the Record of Illness Form – Individual to record information 
(symptoms, age, room, etc.) concerning an ill child.
3. Ask parents to drop-off and pick-up their children at staggered intervals (to avoid interaction).
4. Parents should drop-off children at the main entrance and have the provider bring the child into their 
home. Parents may wait outside the main entrance at pick-up time.
5. Designate an area near the main entrance to conduct screening. The area should allow for physical 
distancing (minimum 2 meters) between provider and the person being screened. If physical distancing 
cannot be maintained, providers conducting screening should wear appropriate personal protective 
equipment, such as mask and gloves. Alcohol based hand rub (ABHR) containing at least 60-90% alcohol 
content should be available at the screening station for hand hygiene.
6. Actively screen parents and children, prior to entry/drop-off for respiratory symptoms. A central 
record of monitoring should be kept. Examples of screening questions include, but not limited to the 
following:
a) Do you or the child or any member of your household have any of the following symptoms:

• Fever
• New or existing cough
• Difficulty breathing
• Sore throat
• Vomiting and/or diarrhea?
b) Have you or the child had close contact (within 2 meters) in the last 14 days with a person with
acute respiratory illness?
c) Have you or the child had close contact (within 2 meters) in the last 14 days with someone that
is a confirmed/ probable case of COVID-19 (including those who are waiting for COVID testing
result)?
d) Have you or the child travelled outside of Canada, including the United States, within the last 14
days?
7. Anyone who answers YES to any of the question should not be permitted to enter the provider's home.
8. Make ABHR available at the screening stations for people who have answered NO to all questions for 
use prior to entry into the home. When ABHR is used on children they must be supervised. To ensure  
proper use, follow the manufacturer’s directions.
9. Thermometers must be covered with single-use protective covers (which is disposed after each use) or
cleaned and disinfected with an outbreak level disinfectant before reuse.
10. Post signage in visible areas which clearly explains the screening process and the rules and conditions for
entry.
11. Handwashing for children will be done immediately upon arrival.

1. Use alternative hooŬs (so that children’s clothes and objects do not touch each other).
Activities 
1. It is ok to explain the current situation to children, without unduly worrying them, in a language
understandable to them: https://www.huffingtonpost.ca/entry/coronavirus-kids-

2. Keep at least a 2-meter distance between children when conducting activities.
3. Reduce the number children gathering in any learning area.
4. Move children amongst learning areas while the other areas are disinfected.
5. Avoid activities that require children to share toys or touch each other (holding hands etc.). If playing
board games, let only one child move the pieces.
6. Limit group sizes (E.g. washroom – do not line up).

8. Handwashing will be made a new routine and fun-activity which will be conducted at every hour/two-
hour interval, or more frequently.
Interactions with Infants and Toddlers 
1. Provider must continue to supervise and hold bottles for infants not yet able to hold their own 
bottle to reduce the risk of choking.
2. When holding infants and toddlers use blankets or cloths over clothing and change the blankets or
cloths between children.
3. Consider removing cribs or placing infants in every other crib and mark the cribs that should not be used
to support physical distancing.
4. Recognizing that physical distancing is difficult with small children and infants, suggestions to support
physical distancing include:
a) Planning activities that do not involve shared objects or toys; and,
b) When possible, moving activities outside to allow for more space.
5. Children must not share food, feeding utensils, soothers, bottles, sippy cups, etc. Mouthed toys must be
removed immediately for cleaning and disinfecting and must not be shared with other children.
6. Label these items with the child’s name to discourage accidental sharing. 
Ratios 
1. Follow the ratios rules and room capacity rules as mandated by Childcare Licensing.
Snack and Mealtimes 
1. Follow all existing rules for cleaning and disinfection and hand hygiene before, during and after meals.
2. Keep one chair distance apart at snack and mealtimes when children are eating.
3. Providers must wear gloves when serving food.
4. Use disposable cutlery where possible.
5. There should be no sharing of utensils.
6. All food preparation and portioning, including snacks, must be done in the kitchen. 
7. There is no self-serve or sharing of food. Provider serves all food to the children.
8. Limit the number of personal water bottles. Use cups instead and send them to be washed immediately.
Use paper cups if available.
9. Food remains should not be reused but thrown in the garbage right way.
10. Provider must return dirty dishes and cutlery to the sinŬ Ĩor disinĨecting.

1. Ensure that all areas (including play material areas) that you will be using the next day are

Provision of Special Needs Resources (SNR) Services 
1. Childcare Licensing recognizes that children with special needs and their families continue to require
additional supports and services in childcare settings.
2. The provision of in-person special needs services in childcare settings should continue where
appropriate. Should questions arise in respect of which service providers are permitted to enter the
premises, please consult with your local public health unit. Please work with special needs service
providers to explore alternative modes of service delivery where in-person delivery is not possible.
3. Maximum capacity rules do not apply to SNR staff (consultants and enhanced staff) on site (i.e., if they
are not counted towards staff to child ratios, they are not included in the maximum capacity rules).
4. Where SNR services are provided through external service providers, licensees and home childcare 
providers should inform all families of this fact, and record attendance for contact tracing purposes.
5. All SNR staff must be screened before entering the childcare setting, as per the protocol in the screening
section above.

Cleaning and Disinfection
Increase Frequency of Cleaning and Disinfecting 
1. Twice daily - High-touch areas – doorknobs, handles, phones, tablets (iPads), light switches.
2. Daily - Counters, chairs, tables, toilets, and sinks (after each child has sat/used that area).
3. Put away toy for cleaning and disinfecting as soon as it is mouthed.
4. Floors in the home with children are cleaned twice a day. And once a day for floors without
children (e.g. kitchen, hallways).
5. bathrooms, and common areas disinfected and cleaned frequently. 
Home Cleaning 
1. Follow Public Health Requirements OR
2. Unless specified here, double cleaning procedures based on existing All Child Care Solutions Inc. requirements.
Toys and learning Material 
1. Toys and learning material must be cleaned and disinfected at least once per day.
2. Limit the number of learning materials to use. E.g. in Dramatic area reduce the number of cups, or
dolls.
3. Remove materials that are difficult to clean – natural materials, dramatic play clothes, stuffed toys.
4. Replace with toys that you can possibly disinfect in a dishwasher.
5. Quarantine toys that you are unable to clean (put it in a plastic bag, label).
6. Toys and equipment should be cleaned and disinfected at a minimum daily͊
7. Have designated toys and equipment (e.g., balls, loose equipment) for each child. Where toys and
equipment are shared, they should be cleaned and disinfected prior to being shared.
8. If sensory materials (e.g., playdough, water, sand, etc.) are offered, they should be provided for
single use (i.e. available to the KE child for the day) and labelled with child’s name͘
Other Areas & Items 
1. Coat Zoom – clean and disinfect at the end of each day.
2. COTS – clean and disinfect at the end of each day.
3. SHEETS, covers, blankets – Wash and dry as required.
4. Where applicable, provide a cup of ‘Clean’ pens for parents to use when signing the Sign-in Sheet
upon dropping off their child. Place the used pens in a cup marked ‘Used’. ‘Used’ pens must be
disinfected before placing back in the ‘Clean’ cup. Alternatively, ask parents to use their personal
pen.
Use the Cleaning Schedule Form to track your cleaning routines so not to miss anything. As much as possible, 
cleaning and disinfecting must be carried out with children not in the room, or in a separate room.

Monitoring and Communicating 
1. Ensure providers are carrying out their enhanced routines regularly and correctly.
2. Ensure you can communicate with local Public Health in a timely manner for any issues that emerge.
3. Do not hesitate to reach out to the local Public Health Department for directions on situations.
Preventive Procedures for Home Visitors and Directors 
1. Director/Home Visitor must be in contact as much as possible to ensure compliance with the 
instructions to parents, children, and prioviders.
2. Stay in the office as much as possible and make sure to follow up with the team working with children while

Preventive Procedures for Suppliers

3. Inform suppliers that you must apply strict measures to limit the spread of the virus.
4. If you must buy food at the grocery store, encourage online shopping and delivery. When you receive the 
delivery, limit handling or movement in the home. Clean the surfaces that have been in contact with items.
