All Child Care Solutions Inc. 
Program Statement 
Home	Child-Care	
All Child Care Solutions Inc.	provides	parents	with	high	quality	daycare	in	Toronto,	Durham	
Region	and	York	Region.	Our	organization	has	done	so	for	over	30	years.	We	are	fully	licensed	
by	the	Ministry	of	Education.	Our	qualified	child-care	providers	invite	your	children	into	their	
homes	to	learn,	play	and	explore	the	world	around	them.	
The	home	is	where	children	begin	to	learn,	by	observing	and	replicating	what	their	parents	are	
doing.	From	their	first	step,	to	their	first	word	and	until	their	first	day	at	school,	parents	are	the	
main	educators	of	children.	Our	providers	duplicate	this	natural	home	environment	as	they	
care	for	and	encourage	the	children	to	learn.	
All Child Care Solutions Inc.	arranges	child-care	in	the	homes	of	independent	providers	who	are	
licensed	under	the	agency’s	umbrella.	The	children	play	and	learn	in	a	cozy	domestic	
environment	that	is	safe	and	nurturing.	The	providers	follow	the	guidelines	in	Ontario	Ministry	
of	Education’s	How	Does	Learning	Happened?	Ontario’s	Pedagogy	for	the	Early	Years.	This	
program	statement	outlines	how	our	agency	works	with	providers,	children,	parents	and	the	
community	to	ensure	that	children	learn	every	day,	according	to	the	government	guidelines.

Core	Principles	-	Discovery	Through	Play	
Our	providers	ensure	that	the	well-being	of	the	children	is	their	key	priority	in	the	home.	That	
is,	the	children	are	safe,	eat	well,	are	nurtured	and	stimulated.	When	children	have	these	basic	
needs	met,	they	are	more	likely	to	self-regulate,	and	to	positively	communicate	and	interact	
with	each	other.	
Play	is	the	primary	way	that	all	children	learn.	Each	day	our	providers	create	play-based	
learning	experiences	centred	on	the	children’s	interests.	These	experiences	are	holistic	and	
encourage	learning	in	all	areas	of	development	–	the	physical,	social,	emotional	as	well	as	
cognitive.		
We	view	all	children	as	competent,	capable,	curious	and	rich	in	potential.	All	of	these	qualities	
are	essential	for	learning,	and	our	providers	encourage	them	in	the	children.	Play	is	not	just	fun.	
It	allows	children	to	experiment,	to	make	and	test	hypotheses,	as	well	as	to	know	themselves.			
Children	are	naturally	curious.	We	boost	this	by	supplying	our	homes	with	books,	toys	and	
open-ended	learning	materials.	Our	providers	rotate	these	frequently	so	that	the	children	get	
plenty	of	stimulation.	Traditional	toys	may	include	trains,	dolls	and	building	blocks.	Open-ended	
materials	may	include	cardboard	tubes,	cotton	wool	balls	and	coloured	strings.	A	child	can	be	
creative	and	turn	these	into	art,	a	space	ship	or	a	mermaid’s	hair.		
What	Will	Your	Child	Be	Doing?	
Each	day	your	child	will	do	all	the	things	that	they	want	to	do.	This	may	include	story-telling,	
playing	with	the	blocks,	opportunities	to	count	and	compare,	to	make	pictures,	sing	and	to	
make-believe.	The	children	initiate	much	of	the	activities	for	each	day.	They	are	encouraged	to	
experience	the	world	by	exploring,	investigating	and	through	self-regulating,	inside	the	home	
and	outdoors.		
Local	neighbourhood	walks	extends	learning	from	the	home,	to	the	outdoors	and	reaches	out	
to	the	community.	As	a	learning	environment	the	outdoors	provides	many	opportunities	for	
physical	exercise,	exploring	nature,	and	for	independent	and	cooperative	games.	When	the	
weather	is	inclement	and	unpredictable,	alternative	activities	for	the	children	are	provided.	In	
addition,	our	providers	may	take	the	children	on	trips	to	the	library	and	to	the	drop-in	programs	
at	the	Ontario	Early	Years	Centre.		
Some	of	our	providers	have	gardens	where	the	children	are	encouraged	to	plant	seeds	and	to	
watch	them	grow.	Children	learn	about	the	environment	and	different	forms	of	life	by	digging	
in	the	soil.	They	learn	about	the	web	of	life	from	watching	birds,	snails	and	butterflies	in	the	
garden.	They	enjoy	the	freedom	of	movement;	get	plenty	of	exercise	from	running	around	
outside	and	from	using	the	play	structures.	Depending	on	their	interest	and	energy	level,	
children	may	spend	many	hours	outside.	Providers	who	do	not	have	a	backyard,	take	the

children	for	walks	in	the	neighbourhood	and	to	nearby	parks.	Many	of	these	parks	have	splash	
pads,	with	supervisory	staff,	where	the	children	can	play	in	the	water	in	the	summer.	In	colder	
weather	the	children	may	still	spend	time	outdoors	to	play,	observe	the	changing	seasons	and	
to	get	some	fresh	air	and	exercise.		
Our	providers	ensure	that	the	children	eat	balanced	meals	each	day.	All	the	foods	are	prepared	
by	the	providers	and	are	based	on	the	Canada	Food	Guide.	The	meals	always	contain	lots	of	
fresh	fruits	and	vegetables.	The	menu	may	change	each	month	to	encourage	the	children	to	try	
new	foods.	The	providers	eat	with	the	children,	encouraging	them	to	feed	themselves	and	to	
practice	their	social	skills	around	the	dining	table.	
Rest	times	allow	children	to	reflect	and	consolidate	their	learnings.	Infants	rest	according	to	
their	own	individual	schedules.	Toddler	and	preschool	children	can	rest	up	to	two	hours	per	
day.	Quiet	activities	are	offered	to	children	as	they	transition	from	rest	to	the	afternoon	
playtime.	
The	Role	Of	Our	Child-Care	Providers	
Each	day	our	providers	observe,	facilitate,	document	and	research	how	the	children	are	
learning.	Each	provider	can	care	for	a	maximum	of	six	children,	from	birth	to	13	years,	in	their	
homes.	The	small	ratio	means	that	each	child	gets	individual	attention.	Our	providers	are	
trained	to	watch	how	the	children	play	each	day.	From	these	observations,	the	provider	may	
suggest	activities	and	offer	materials	that	encourage	the	children	to	explore,	experiment	and	
use	their	imaginations.		
In	essence	our	providers’	role	is	to:	
● Establish	a	social	environment	that	supports	your	child’s	autonomy	and	self-esteem.
● Establish	positive	relationships,	friendships	and	resolve	conflicts.
● Provide	responsive	care	to	all	children	while	meeting	their	individual	needs.
● Provide	a	safe	physical	environment.
● Provide	new	play	possibilities	through	interest	areas.
● Be	an	involved	play	partner.
● Foster,	observe	and	document	significant	behaviour/developmental	milestones.
● Incorporate	the	community.

What	Will	The	Provider	Be	Doing?	
We	view	a	positive,	supportive	relationship	between	child	and	provider	as	fundamental.	Our	
providers:	
● Support	you	in	your	role	as	a	parent.
● Provide	a	safe	environment	for	your	child	and	create	spaces	that	are	conducive	to	learning.
● Connect	with	children	by	being	available,	sensitive,	responsive	and	caring.	This	way	the
providers	build	trust,	and	make	the	children	feel	safe	and	secure.	Once	this	base	relationship
is	established	children	are	more	likely	to	take	risks,	grow	and	learn.
● Watch	carefully	to	determine	what	your	child,	at	that	particular	moment,	is	capable	of
learning.
● Actively	participate	in	your	child’s	play	experiences,	and	observe	and	document	the
milestones	in	your	child’s	life	to	ensure	age-appropriate	development.
Program	Assessment	and	Reflective	Practices	
Our	providers	reflect	and	assess	their	activities,	programs	and	environment	daily,	to	ensure	that	
they	are	meeting	the	individual	needs	of	the	children,	and	the	interests	of	the	group.	
The	providers	keep	a	journal	for	each	child.	In	it,	they	document	how	the	child	is	learning;	the	
journal	is	shared	with	parents	at	the	end	of	each	month.	Our	providers	may	also	take	
photographs	and	cellphone	videos	of	the	children,	as	these	are	other	ways	of	documenting	how	
and	what	the	children	are	learning.	The	photos	and	videos	are	shared	with	parents	so	that	they	
can	try	similar	activities	at	home	or	just	to	see	the	achievements	of	their	child.	Providers	will	
also	observe	and	document	challenges	faced	by	the	child	and	what	strategies	they	will	use	to	
help	the	child	deal	with	these	challenges.	These	strategies	encourage	on-going	communication	
with	parents	and	help	to	keep	them	engaged	in	how	their	child	is	learning.	
Our	providers	are	competent,	professional	and	knowledgeable.	They	attend	frequent	seminars	
and	workshops	to	keep	up	with	the	latest	changes	and	research	in	the	child-care	field.		
The	agency’s	home	visitors	review	activities,	program	plans	and	pedagogical	documents	to	
ensure	that	they	meet	the	agency’s	requirements.	Our	home	visitors	demonstrate	pedagogical	
leadership	by	observing	each	home	monthly	and	engaging	in	conversation	with	the	providers	
and	children	regarding	how	the	children	are	learning,	what	they	need	to	learn	and	what	is	the	
best	means	for	this	learning	to	happen.	Quarterly,	our	home	visitors	complete	a	licensing	
checklist	for	each	home	and	its	programs	to	ensure	that	the	agency’s	quality	indicators	have	
been	met.
