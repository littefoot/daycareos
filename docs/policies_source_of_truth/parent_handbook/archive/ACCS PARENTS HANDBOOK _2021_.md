BEHAVIOUR MANAGEMENT PRACTICES

When we screen providers, we consider their attitudes towards child discipline and management.

Behaviour management principles and methods are discussed during provider orientation and followed up at

workshop and training sessions.

Children are disciplined in a positive manner at a level that is appropriate to their actions and ages. (Ex. Removal from the current play area to do another activity). We feel that our major goal in the area of discipline is (self-control-helping the child take steps toward responsible self-direction). Our staff members do not use these

Prohibited Practices:

(a) corporal punishment of the child;

(b) physical restraint of the child, such as confining the child to a high chair, car seat, stroller or other device for the purposes of discipline or in lieu of supervision, unless the physical restraint is for the purpose of preventing a child from hurting himself, herself or someone else, and is used only as a last resort and only until the risk of injury is no longer imminent;

(c) locking the exits of the child care centre or home child care premises for the purpose of confining the child, or confining the child in an area or room without adult supervision, unless such confinement occurs during an emergency and is required as part of the licensee’s emergency management policies and procedures;

(d) use of harsh or degrading measures or threats or use of derogatory language directed at or used in the presence of a child that would humiliate, shame or frighten the child or undermine his or her self-respect, dignity or self-worth;

(e) depriving the child of basic needs including food, drink, shelter, sleep, toilet use, clothing or bedding; or

(f) inflicting any bodily harm on children including making children eat or drink against their will. O. Reg. 126/16, s. 34.

Children have a right to feel challenged in response to restrictions or interference. Our children need to feel that they can assert themselves safely, and at the same time, know that they can live with restrictions and limitations

Parents own views and philosophies on child management can be discussed at the initial meeting between parents and the prospective provider. Our policies require that the provider inform the parent of any behavioural problems or disciplinary action involving your child(ren) when they are picked up. This maintains appropriate transparency and extended support for families.

29 PARENT ISSUES AND CONCERNS

This Agency will not tolerate any discrimination, physical, verbal, (sexual) harassment or inappropriate behavior. We maintain a policy of commitment and equality. All policies of the Agency are presented up front to anyone willing to get involved with this Agency. All complaints or concerns must be brought to the attention of the Agency Director.

Parents/guardians are encouraged to take an active role in our child care organization and regularly discuss what their child(ren) are experiencing with our program. As supported by our program statement, we support positive and responsive interactions among the children, parents/guardians, child care providers and staff, and foster the engagement of and ongoing communication with parents/guardians about the program and their children. All issues and concerns raised by parents/guardians are taken seriously by All Child Care Solutions Inc. and will be addressed. Every effort will be made to address and resolve issues and concerns to the satisfaction of all parties and as quickly as possible.

Issues/concerns may be brought forward verbally or in writing. Responses and outcomes will be provided verbally, or in writing upon request. The level of detail provided to the parent/guardian will respect and maintain the confidentiality of all parties involved.

An initial response to an issue or concern will be provided to parents/guardians within All Child Care Solutions Inc.’s business day(s). The person who raised the issue/concern will be kept informed throughout the resolution process.

Investigations of issues and concerns will be fair, impartial and respectful to parties involved.

Confidentiality

Every issue and concern will be treated confidentially and every effort will be made to protect the privacy of parents/guardians, children, staff, students and volunteers, except when information must be disclosed for legal reasons (e.g. to the Ministry of Education, College of Early Childhood Educators, law enforcement authorities or a Children’s Aid Society).

Conduct

Our Organization maintains high standards for positive interaction, communication and role-modeling for children. Harassment and discrimination will therefore not be tolerated from any party.

If at any point a parent/guardian, provider or staff feels uncomfortable, threatened, abused or belittled, they may immediately end the conversation and report the situation to the supervisor and/or licensee/Directors.

Concerns about the Suspected Abuse or Neglect of a child

Everyone, including members of the public and professionals who work closely with children, is required by law to report suspected cases of child abuse or neglect.

If a parent/guardian expresses concerns that a child is being abused or neglected, the parent will be advised to contact the local Children’s Aid Society (CAS) directly.

Persons who become aware of such concerns are also responsible for reporting this information to CAS as per the “Duty to Report” requirement under the Child and Family Services Act. 39 WAITING LIST

This policy and the procedures within provide for waiting lists to be administered in a transparent manner. It supports the availability of information about the waiting list for prospective parents in a way that maintains the privacy and confidentiality of children.

The procedures provide steps that will be followed to place children on the waiting list, offer admission, and provide parents with information about their child’s position on the waiting list.

This policy is intended to fulfill the obligations set out under Ontario Regulation 137/15 for a child care agency that maintains a waiting list to have related policies and procedures.

Policy:

General

All Child Care Solutions Inc. will strive to accommodate all requests for the registration of a child at the child care home.  Where the maximum capacity of a home has been reached and spaces are unavailable for new children to be enrolled, the waiting list procedures set out below will be followed.

No fee will be charged to parents for placing a child on the waiting list.

Procedures:

Receiving a Request to Place a Child on the Waiting List

The licensee will receive parental requests to place children on a waiting list via [email, phone call and in-person meet & greet.)

Each family who wishes to be placed on the waitlist must fill out All Child Care Solutions Inc. Application Form

Placing a Child on the Waiting List

The licensee will place a child on the waiting list in chronological order, based on the date and time that the request was received.

Each family who wishes to be placed on the waitlist must fill out All Child Care Solutions Inc. Application Form.

Once a child has been placed on the waiting list, the licensee will inform parents of their child’s position on the list.

Determining Placement Priority when a Space Becomes Available

When space becomes available at the home of request, a one month’s deposit will be required to hold your spot. Deposits are per child, a discount may be offered for one than one child placed. The deposit is only refundable within two weeks of making the deposit.

Offering an Available Space

40 PROGRAM STATEMENT

ALL CHILD CARE SOLUTION INC. provides parents with high quality daycare in Toronto, Durham Region and York Region. Our organization has done so for over 30 years. We are fully licensed by the Ministry of Education. Our qualified child-care providers invite your children into their homes to learn, play and explore the world around them.

The home is where children begin to learn, by observing and replicating what their parents are doing. From their first step, to their first word and until their first day at school, parents are the main educators of children. Our providers duplicate this natural home environment as they care for and encourage the children to learn.

ALL CHILD CARE SOLUTION INC. arranges child-care in the homes of independent providers who are licensed under the agency’s umbrella. The children play and learn in a cozy domestic environment that is safe and nurturing. The providers follow the guidelines in Ontario Ministry of Education’s How Does Learning Happened? Ontario’s Pedagogy for the Early Years. This program statement outlines how our agency works with providers, children, parents and the community to ensure that children learn every day, according to the government guidelines.

Core Principles - Discovery Through Play

Our providers ensure that the well-being of the children is their key priority in the home. That is, the children are safe, eat well, are nurtured and stimulated. When children have these basic needs met, they are more likely to self-regulate, and to positively communicate and interact with each other.

Play is the primary way that all children learn. Each day our providers create play-based learning experiences centred on the children’s interests. These experiences are holistic and encourage learning in all areas of development – the physical, social, emotional as well as cognitive.

We view all children as competent, capable, curious and rich in potential. All of these qualities are essential for learning, and our providers encourage them in the children. Play is not just fun. It allows children to experiment, to make and test hypotheses, as well as to know themselves.

Children are naturally curious. We boost this by supplying our homes with books, toys and open-ended learning materials. Our providers rotate these frequently so that the children get plenty of stimulation. Traditional toys may include trains, dolls and building blocks. Open-ended materials may include cardboard tubes, cotton wool balls and coloured strings. A child can be creative and turn these into art, a space ship or a mermaid’s hair.

What Will Your Child Be Doing?

Each day your child will do all the things that they want to do. This may include story-telling, playing with the blocks, opportunities to count and compare, to make pictures, sing and to make-believe. The children initiate much of the activities for each day. They are encouraged to experience the world by exploring, investigating and through self-regulating, inside the home and outdoors.

Local neighbourhood walks extends learning from the home, to the outdoors and reaches out to the community. As a learning environment the outdoors provides many opportunities for physical exercise, exploring nature, and for independent and cooperative games. When the weather is inclement and unpredictable, alternative activities for the children are provided. In addition, our providers may take the children on trips to the library and to the drop-in programs at the Ontario Early Years Centre.

Some of our providers have gardens where the children are encouraged to plant seeds and to watch them grow. Children learn about the environment and different forms of life by digging in the soil. They learn about the web of life from watching birds, snails and butterflies in the garden. They enjoy the freedom of movement; get plenty of exercise from running around outside and from using the play structures. Depending on their interest and energy level, children may spend many hours outside. Providers who do not have a backyard, take the children for walks in the neighbourhood and to nearby parks. Many of these parks have splash pads, with supervisory staff, where the children can play in the water in the summer. In colder weather the children may still spend time outdoors to play, observe the changing seasons and to get some fresh air and exercise.

Our providers ensure that the children eat balanced meals each day. All the foods are prepared by the providers and are based on the Canada Food Guide. The meals always contain lots of fresh fruits and vegetables. The menu may change each month to encourage the children to try new foods. The providers eat with the children, encouraging them to feed themselves and to practice their social skills around the dining table.

Rest times allow children to reflect and consolidate their learnings. Infants rest according to their own individual schedules. Toddler and preschool children can rest up to two hours per day. Quiet activities are offered to children as they transition from rest to the afternoon playtime.

The Role Of Our Child-Care Providers

Each day our providers observe, facilitate, document and research how the children are learning. Each provider can care for a maximum of six children, from birth to 13 years, in their homes. The small ratio means that each child gets individual attention. Our providers are trained to watch how the children play each day. From these observations, the provider may suggest activities and offer materials that encourage the children to explore, experiment and use their imaginations.

In essence our providers’ role is to:

● Establish a social environment that supports your child’s autonomy and self-esteem.

● Establish positive relationships, friendships and resolve conflicts.

● Provide responsive care to all children while meeting their individual needs.

● Provide a safe physical environment.

● Provide new play possibilities through interest areas.

● Be an involved play partner.

● Foster, observe and document significant behaviour/developmental milestones.

● Incorporate the community.

What Will The Provider Be Doing?

We view a positive, supportive relationship between child and provider as fundamental. Our providers:

● Support you in your role as a parent.

● Provide a safe environment for your child and create spaces that are conducive to learning.

● Connect with children by being available, sensitive, responsive and caring. This way the       providers build trust, and make the children feel safe and secure. Once this base relationship is established children are more likely to take risks, grow and learn.

● Watch carefully to determine what your child, at that particular moment, is capable of learning.

● Actively participate in your child’s play experiences, and observe and document the milestones in your child’s life to ensure age-appropriate development.

Program Assessment and Reflective Practices

Our providers reflect and assess their activities, programs and environment daily, to ensure that they are meeting the individual needs of the children, and the interests of the group.

The providers keep a journal for each child. In it, they document how the child is learning; the journal is shared with parents at the end of each month. Our providers may also take photographs and cellphone videos of the children, as these are other ways of documenting how and what the children are learning. The photos and videos are shared with parents so that they can try similar activities at home or just to see the achievements of their child. Providers will also observe and document challenges faced by the child and what strategies they will use to help the child deal with these challenges. These strategies encourage on-going communication with parents and help to keep them engaged in how their child is learning.

Our providers are competent, professional and knowledgeable. They attend frequent seminars and workshops to keep up with the latest changes and research in the child-care field.

The agency’s home visitors review activities, program plans and pedagogical documents to ensure that they meet the agency’s requirements. Our home visitors demonstrate pedagogical leadership by observing each home monthly and engaging in conversation with the providers and children regarding how the children are learning, what they need to learn and what is the best means for this learning to happen. Quarterly, our home visitors complete a licensing checklist for each home and its programs to ensure that the agency’s quality indicators have been met.

In the event of an emergency, such as fire, bomb threat, flood or any other situation in which there is immediate danger to being inside the home, The Provider and All Child Care Solutions Inc. will have designated the _______________________________ as the off-site location.

• Notify families of the situation and evacuation location for immediate pick-up of children.  (home visitor or designate responsible for calling families, etc.)  Make sure you are clear on what information is being given to families and that providers are relaying the same message.

Assisted Evacuation

One-on-one, or additional, help may be required by a child with special needs.

Tips on assisting children with special needs:

• Have specific knowledge of the child’s needs.

• Speak clearly and inform the child of what is happening and what you are going to do.  Once child is aware of how you will help them, proceed by assisting them in the appropriate manor (push/pull wheelchair, etc)

• To communicate with a deaf and blind person, draw an ‘X’ on their back or try tracing letters with your finger on the palm of their hand to let them know you are there to help during the emergency.

• Follow instructions posted on special needs equipment or assistive device during an emergency.

• Avoid attempts to lift, support or assist in moving someone unless you are familiar with safe techniques.

Sheltering

Sheltering involves keeping all windows and doors closed and covering air-intake vents to provide protection from airborne hazardous materials.  You would normally take shelter in the event of earthquakes, hurricanes, tornados, hazardous materials accidents, terrorist attacks, etc.

• Ensure all providers are in the home (outside play areas are unoccupied)

• Ensure all doors and windows are locked

• Turn off all exhaust fans in kitchens, bathrooms and any other spaces

• Use tape and plastic food wrapping to cover bathroom exhausts, vents, dryer vents and other openings to the outdoors.  Seal any obvious gaps around windows and doors.  Stay away from all windows and doors.

• Close the curtains for additional protection

• If the vapors begin to bother the providers and children, hold wet cloths over the nose and mouth.

• Remain in the home until notified by the emergency response authorities that the situation has been resolved.

Strategies to Reduce the Risk of Exposure to Anaphylactic Allergens

The following strategies to reduce the risk of exposure to anaphylactic causative agents must be followed at all times by home visitors of the home child care agency, as well as providers, other persons regularly present or ordinarily resident, students and volunteers at each home child care premises.

·   	Do not serve foods where its ingredients are not known.

·   	Do not serve items with ‘may contain’ warnings on the label in a premises with a child who has an individualized plan and emergency procedures specifying those allergens.

·   	Ensure that parents label food brought to the premises with the child’s full name and the date the food arrived at the premises, and that parents advise of all ingredients.

·   	Where food is provided from home for children, ensure that appropriate supervision of children is maintained so that food is not shared or exchanged.

·   	Encourage parents who serve foods containing allergens at home to ensure their child has been rid of the allergens prior to attending the home child care premises (e.g. by thoroughly washing hands, brushing teeth, etc.)

·   	Do not use craft/sensory materials and toys that have known allergens on the labels.

·   	Share information about anaphylaxis, strategies to reduce the risk of exposure to known allergens and treatment with all families of children receiving care at the premises.

·   	Make sure each child’s individual plan and emergency procedures are kept-up-to-date and that all home child care agency home visitors, providers, other persons regularly present and ordinarily resident, students, and volunteers at the home child care premises are trained on the plans.

·   	Create allergy lists for each home child care premises and ensure that they are up to date in each premises and implemented.

·   	Update providers, other persons regularly present and ordinarily resident, home visitors, students, and volunteers when changes to a child’s allergies, signs and symptoms, and treatment occur and review all updates to individualized plans and emergency procedures.

·   	Update families when changes to allergies occur for a child receiving care at a premises while maintaining the confidentiality of children.

·   	The strategies in this policy will be updated or revised and implemented depending on the allergies of children receiving child care at home child care premises.

Communication Plan

The following is our communication plan for sharing information on life-threatening and anaphylactic allergies with home child care providers, home child care agency home visitors, students, volunteers, parents and families.

·   	Parents will be encouraged not to bring foods to home child care premises that contain ingredients to which children may be allergic

·   	Parents and families will be informed about anaphylactic allergies and all known allergens at the home premises in which their child is placed through Phone, Email, Text.

·   	A list of all children’s allergies including food and other causative agents will be posted in all cooking areas at each home child care premises, and will be made available in any other area where children may be present.

·   	Each child with an anaphylactic allergy who is enrolled at a home child care premises through the home child care agency will have an individualized plan and emergency procedures that detail signs and symptoms specific to the child describing how to identify that they are having an allergic reaction and what to do if they experience a reaction.

·   	Each child’s individualized plan and emergency procedures will be made available and accessible wherever the child may be present while receiving child care.

·   	In cases where a child has food allergies and the meals and snacks provided by the provider cannot meet the child’s needs, the parent may be asked to supply snacks/meals for their child. All written instructions for diet provided by a parent will be implemented.

·   	The home child care agency will communicate with the Ministry of Education by reporting serious occurrences where a life-threatening situation occurs in accordance with the established serious occurrence policy and procedures.

·   	This communication plan will be continually reviewed to ensure it is meeting the needs of the home child care agency and that it is effectively achieving its intended result.
